Straight answers

What owners ask us first.

How close is the picture to the real piece?
Close enough to choose with, and it says so on the glass: a likeness, not a fitting. The colour, the print and where the embroidery sits come from your own photograph; fine embroidery comes out close, not stitch for stitch. Your photo is one tap away beside every look, and any piece you would rather not have drawn can be kept to your photographs only.
Where do the shopper's pictures go?
One frame leaves the screen only when she asks for a look, and the mirror asks her first. It goes to the try-on engine's servers abroad for the seconds it takes, and a store mirror keeps nothing. Your numbers are yours: no other retailer sees them. The privacy notice says it in full.
Will my staff use it, or does it replace them?
It works for them. A salesperson gets three looks to start from and something to show in the first minute; the sale is still theirs, and their name is on it when they tap Sold. Training is twenty minutes.
What if the internet is slow?
A portrait needs very little: when the line will not carry live video, the mirror offers a portrait in one tap. If the line is down it says so in plain words and carries on when it is back. We test your line before anything is installed.
Does it connect to our till or stock?
Not yet. MIRVA reads sizes and stock from your website, and a sale is counted when your salesperson taps Sold, with the amount and their name. A direct till connection is something we would build with a first chain partner.
Who is behind MIRVA?
MIRVA is new and small: a founder in Lahore and a young team, a project by MVP family. That is why the first stores get the founder's own number and a say in what is built next, and why you can start on your own tablets, month to month. What a mirror agreement promises on service, and what happens to the mirror and your money if we could not carry on, is written into it before you pay anything.
