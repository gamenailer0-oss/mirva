Your shoppers, your data

Built for a country where a woman's photograph matters.

  • Nothing is recorded.

    The camera is a mirror. A single frame leaves it only when she asks for a look, and a store mirror stores no portraits.

Straight answers

What people ask us first.

How accurate is the picture?
It is a likeness, good enough to choose with, and it says so on the glass. The colour, the print and where the embroidery sits are taken from your own photograph of the piece; fine embroidery comes out close, not stitch for stitch. Your photograph is one tap away beside every look, and any piece you would rather not have drawn (hand embroidery, a designer's wish) can be kept to your photographs only. It helps a shopper decide what to try; it does not replace the fitting.
What do you need from us?
Your catalogue with one clear front picture of each piece, and a tablet or two. For a mirror, also a wall near where shoppers stop, a power socket, and a wired or strong Wi-Fi connection; we build and install it.
Does it connect to our till or stock system?
Not yet. Today MIRVA reads the sizes and stock on your website, and a sale is counted when your salesperson taps Sold on the tablet, with the amount and their name. A direct connection to a till is something we would build with a first chain partner.
What if the internet drops?
Portraits and live looks need a connection. When the line will not carry live video, the mirror offers a portrait in one tap, which needs far less. If the line is down altogether it says so in plain words and carries on when it is back. We test the store's line before we install anything.
Will my staff use it, or does it replace them?
It works for them. A salesperson gets three looks to start from and something to show in the first minute; the sale is still theirs, and their name is on it in your console. Training is twenty minutes, and there is a one-page guide for the till.
What if the piece she likes is not in this branch?
MIRVA knows what your website says is sold out, not what is on this branch's rail today. Your staff check the rail, as they do now. A link to branch stock is something we would build with a first chain partner.
Who sees our numbers, and where do the pictures go?
Your numbers are yours: no other retailer sees them, and they are never used to sell for another store. A shopper's picture goes to the try-on engine's servers abroad for the seconds it takes to make the look, and a store mirror keeps none. The privacy notice says this in full.
Is it in Urdu?
The mirror speaks English today. Urdu is next, written by a native speaker rather than translated by a machine.
Who is behind MIRVA?
MIRVA is new and small: one founder, in Lahore. That is why the first stores get the founder's phone number and a say in what is built next. It is also why you can start on Assist, which runs on your own tablets, month to month, and stops the day you say. What a mirror agreement promises on service and repairs, and what happens to the mirror and your money if we could not carry on, is written into it before you pay anything.
